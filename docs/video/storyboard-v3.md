# Combo — choices become a shared experience

**Superseded visually by `storyboard-v4.md`.** The user requested artwork focused only on people, the tangible table and experiences, with text, UI and AI suggestions omitted. This earlier version records the real-time interpretation and co-creation concept.

## Film concept

**Different tastes. One trip.**

Two friends gather around an interactive table. They slide tactile souvenir objects into a shared walnut tray. Each addition immediately changes a readable narrative beside it. One person's desire for pauses remains present when the other introduces evening energy. AI suggests a connection neither selected directly; the friends accept and reshape it together.

The product payoff is a shared direction that expresses more than the individual objects. The emotional payoff is recognizing your own preferences while making room for someone else's.

The minute follows one continuous co-creation session. Reflection and a separate travel montage are omitted to give this core mechanism enough time to be understood.

## What the viewer must understand

- **Choices carry meaning:** tea, book, rail and lantern signal what these people are drawn toward in this session. Avoid definitive personality labels or invented psychological certainty.
- **Choices influence the result:** a central tea preference persists when evening energy arrives. Express relative influence through what the narrative preserves, not numeric scores or a weight dashboard.
- **Combination creates new meaning:** tea + book suggests lingering; adding rail suggests movement punctuated by pauses; adding lantern creates a day/evening rhythm. The feedback must connect items rather than merely list them.
- **Feedback is continuous:** show object placement → visible reading change → relevant suggestion. No separate “generate” step. These are target behavior and illustrative responses; final footage must reflect actual latency and output.
- **People and AI co-create:** both friends contribute, AI proposes a contextual possibility, and the people decide whether to include it and how to arrange it. They remain active throughout.

## Tangible table direction

Use the supplied reference's stone, walnut, ceramic, glass, paper, natural light, cream panels, forest-green controls and editorial type. Frame it as a shared horizontal touch surface: rendered souvenirs and tray lie on the screen plane, and fingertips select and slide them. The surface should invite tactile interaction.

This is the user's tangible-table experience direction, not a specification for object-recognition hardware or a claim that force-feedback haptics exist. No sensor recognition, robotic AI, holograms or weight-meter graphics are needed to communicate the idea. Convey touch through contact, object settling, material sound and the people's reactions; use actual haptic behavior in the finished film only if implemented.

## Edit and sound

Start with both people and the product in frame. Alternate over-shoulder views with close tabletop shots; keep the tray's orientation consistent so the growing collection remains legible. Use each arriving object to motivate a cut. Briefly retain the previous wording as the new reading replaces it, making the change visible without a technical diagram.

Hold each important reading for a few seconds. The noodle suggestion remains outside the tray until accepted. Keep all five selected objects in the final state. Human expressions should show recognition, negotiation and shared excitement.

Sound: a light warm pulse, small ceramic/paper contact sounds, soft interface confirmation and a brief shared laugh. Narration supports visible behavior rather than explaining backend architecture. Add final typography and motion in the edit; generated lettering is concept art.
